Management conclusion

Severity tells you how bad the issue is. Impact tells you how much of the account it can touch.The transformation delay is “High” technically, but its account impact is 4.8/5 because it crosses delivery, value, relationship, renewal, and growth.
Decision readout

An escalation tower should not be a ticket list. It should expose the connection between an issue, its business impact, the dependencies around it, and the owner accountable for closure.

The transformation delay is the best example. Its technical severity is High, but its account impact outranks the Critical outage in this synthetic model because the delay propagates into value realization, customer confidence, a €500K expansion opportunity, and renewal risk.

Control loop

Risk → issue → escalation → action → owner → deadline → resolution.

1 · DetectIdentify the issue and quantify customer or commercial impact.
2 · PrioritizeUse account impact and urgency, not technical severity alone.
3 · AssignMake the owner and dependency explicit.
4 · EscalateBring leadership in when cross-functional coordination or customer confidence is at risk.
5 · Close and learnRecord the resolution and feed recurring causes back into process improvement.
Connection to the operating model

The same issue can be simultaneously a delivery problem, an account problem, a renewal problem, and a pipeline problem.

That is why escalation management sits across the customer lifecycle. The control tower makes the dependencies visible so one team does not close its ticket while the account remains strategically exposed.
