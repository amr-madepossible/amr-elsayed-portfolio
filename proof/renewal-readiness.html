Management conclusion

70 is not “70% likely to renew.”It means enough positive conditions exist to plan from, but multiple barriers remain changeable while six months of runway remain.
Decision readout

Renewal readiness is narrower than account health. A customer may use the service, have a good sponsor, and still be poorly prepared to renew if value is unproven, procurement has not started, pricing is unresolved, or a competitor is under consideration.

At 70/100, (A) Group is neither a clean “safe” nor an emergency. It is a managed-risk renewal with enough runway to change the outcome. The right response is a barrier-removal plan, not a prettier red/green label.

The strongest analytical move

Ask the decision that matters: “What could prevent yes?”

Retention barrierIncident

A visible unresolved failure can overwhelm otherwise healthy operating performance.

Evidence barrierValue proof

Usage does not automatically prove the business outcome the customer is paying for.

Commercial barrierProcurement + pricing

A strong relationship can still hit a commercial process wall late in the cycle.

Analytical chain

Score, diagnose, sequence, act.

1 · Score readinessMake evidence requirements explicit across usage, delivery, value, sponsorship, commercial, procurement and competition.
2 · Identify barriersFind the conditions that could block the renewal decision.
3 · Sequence by timeResolve barriers whose lead time could exceed the remaining renewal runway.
4 · Assign ownersEvery barrier becomes an accountable action, not a passive risk statement.
Management lensRetention readiness
Decision ruleTreat the score as a readiness index, not a renewal probability, then manage the barriers that make the score low.
Typical outputRenewal scorecard · risk register · renewal action plan
